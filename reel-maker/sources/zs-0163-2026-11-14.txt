Overview
The whole case on one page.
Independent restaurants rely on third-party aggregators for up to 40% of their order volume, surrendering 25% to 32% in marketplace commissions. While platforms generate initial order flow, operators surrender diner contact data and subsidise aggregator market share at negative net margins. Attempts to move diners to direct ordering routinely fail because independent venues lack frictionless first-party dispatch and automated migration workflows.
researcher note · [modeled]
Independent restaurateurs will pay $179 to $299 per month for an automated channel-shift engine that pairs packaging-driven loyalty capture with white-label delivery dispatch, provided it migrates at least 14% of marketplace orders to first-party channels within 90 days. Downgrade if white-label fleet unit economics equal marketplace take-rates or POS incumbents bundle native migration tools for free.
The case in 30 seconds
who bleeds Independent full-service and fast-casual restaurant operators managing 1 to 4 locations with $600k–$2.2M gross annual revenue per site.
the bleed annual gross commission surrendered per location on marketplace delivery orders
the gap Online ordering providers supply static web menus but leave customer acquisition and marketplace migration entirely to the busy operator, lacking physical-to-digital capture mechanics and automated dispatch routing.
the wedge A thermal-receipt and bag-insert QR generator integrated with POS dispatch that delivers instant personalised discounts to first-time marketplace customers, routing repeat delivery via local white-label couriers.
the endgame The default margin-optimisation layer for independent hospitality, controlling direct diner relationships, automated menu pricing arbitrage, and multi-carrier delivery routing.
the window 18-36 months, driven by tightening restaurant net margins, aggregator fee transparency legislation, and the maturation of white-label delivery infrastructure.
